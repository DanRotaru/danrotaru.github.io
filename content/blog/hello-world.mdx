Hello, world 👋

This is the first post on the new dan13.me. If you've been here before, you'll notice everything looks different. The old site did its job for a while, but it was small, a little stiff, and its blog page said "Coming soon..." for more than two years. So I rebuilt the whole thing, and this time the blog actually has something in it.

This post covers why I did it, what's on the new site, and how it compares to the old one, with screenshots of both.

## Why rebuild it

I launched the old dan13.me in July 2024. It was a Nuxt 3 app with five pages: Home, About, Projects, Toolbox, and a Blog that never got a single post. I put it together quickly, pushed the generated files to GitHub Pages, and moved on to client work.

A lot has changed since then. I'm a Full-Stack Engineer at Lislex now, I build EiHAI on the side, and most of my day involves AI coding agents. The old site didn't say any of that. It also had a few things I wasn't proud of: a meta tag with a hundred-odd SEO keywords, Google Analytics loaded on every page, and a layout that was basically a hero, a photo and five icons.

I wanted a site that feels like me: personal, a bit playful, with room for writing.

## What's new

- **More pages.** The old site had 5. The new one has About, Projects, Blog, Toolbox, Links, Contact, a Coffee page, and the legal pages.
- **A real blog.** Posts are MDX files with syntax-highlighted code, callouts, diagrams, a floating table of contents and an FAQ section. Every post also has a plain Markdown version (add `/index.md` to the URL), there's an RSS feed at `/blog/rss.xml`, and `/llms.txt` lists everything for AI assistants.
- **No tracking scripts.** Google Analytics is gone.
- **Still fully static.** `nuxt generate` builds plain HTML, CSS and JS. No server, no database.

## How it's built

The new site runs on Nuxt 4 and Vue 3.5 with strict TypeScript. I first sketched it in Next.js, then ported it to Nuxt, because Vue is where I'm most at home. Styles are plain SCSS with BEM class names and a small set of design tokens for colors, radii and fonts. No CSS framework.

Blog posts are MDX files. I wired `@mdx-js/rollup` into Vite with a small custom JSX runtime so MDX compiles straight to Vue components. Code blocks are highlighted at build time with Shiki through `rehype-pretty-code`, with one theme for light mode and one for dark. Animations use `motion-v`.

The whole site is generated ahead of time, so hosting it is just a matter of uploading a folder.
